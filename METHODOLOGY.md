# VC Answer Map methodology

Version: `0.1-prebaseline`  
Status: pre-registered pilot protocol  
Owner: Refinery Media  
First baseline: September 2026

## 1. Research question

When a founder describes a company or operating problem to an AI model, which venture capital firms and individual investors does the model recommend, and what explanation does it provide?

The benchmark studies the representation of venture firms in AI answers. It does not measure investment returns, founder satisfaction, partner quality or the objective suitability of a firm for any particular company.

## 2. Why this matters

Founders increasingly use conversational systems to construct fundraising lists, research investors and solve operating problems. A generated answer compresses a large information environment into a small number of recommendations. Firms omitted from that answer may be absent at the moment a founder forms a shortlist or adopts a framework.

Conventional search visibility, live AI retrieval and model knowledge are related but distinct:

1. **Closed-book recognition:** the model associates a firm with a category or problem without live web search.
2. **Open-book discoverability:** a search-enabled system retrieves current sources and surfaces the firm.
3. **Answer inclusion:** the firm or investor appears in the generated recommendation.
4. **Intellectual attribution:** a model uses a specific idea and correctly identifies its originator.

The study measures these separately.

## 3. Pre-registered hypotheses

The first baseline tests six competing hypotheses.

### H1: Prestige concentration

A small group of prominent multi-stage firms will appear across categories, including prompts where specialist firms may have stronger relevance.

### H2: Portfolio evidence

Models will associate firms with investment categories more reliably than with the ideas those firms publish.

### H3: Problem ownership

Some investors or firms will repeatedly appear for specific founder problems because their published frameworks have become durable reference points.

### H4: Individual dominance

Models will associate operating ideas with named investors more precisely than with the institutions where those ideas were developed or published.

### H5: Attribution decay

Some frameworks will appear in answers after their original attribution has weakened or disappeared.

### H6: Retrieval gap

Specialist and emerging firms will appear more often with live search enabled than in closed-book answers.

The study may reject all six hypotheses. Findings will be reported whether or not they support the motivating thesis.

## 4. Experimental units

The primary experimental unit is one response generated by one identified model for one fixed prompt under one declared search condition.

Each record must contain:

- Run identifier
- Timestamp in UTC
- Prompt identifier and exact prompt text
- Prompt type
- Exact model identifier
- Provider and routing metadata where available
- Search condition
- Sampling parameters
- Response text
- Parsed recommendations
- Token usage, latency and cost
- Retry and error history
- Hashes for the prompt file, configuration and runner

## 5. Prompt universe

The pilot contains 20 founder-intent prompts split into two equal groups.

### 5.1 Fund discovery prompts

These ask which firms fit a founder based on combinations of sector, stage, geography, capital intensity or investment philosophy.

1. European seed-stage therapeutics
2. European university deep-technology spinout
3. Capital-intensive climate hardware
4. Seed-stage developer infrastructure
5. Enterprise AI
6. European defence technology
7. Financial infrastructure
8. Consumer technology with distribution and cultural requirements
9. Pre-seed first-time technical founder
10. Capital-efficient B2B software

### 5.2 Founder problem prompts

These ask which venture firms or individual investors have published useful thinking about a specific operating decision.

1. Recognising product-market fit
2. Raising another round or pursuing profitability
3. Calculating whether a company is default alive
4. Finding the first 10 customers
5. Building enterprise sales before hiring a VP of Sales
6. Running an effective board meeting
7. Replacing a senior executive
8. Founder-led sales
9. Sizing a market that does not yet exist
10. Deciding whether venture capital is appropriate

The exact machine-readable prompts live in [`config/prompts.json`](config/prompts.json). Prompt wording remains frozen during a baseline version.

## 6. Model panel

The pilot uses one current model from each of eight developer families available through a common API layer:

- OpenAI
- Anthropic
- Google
- xAI
- Meta Llama
- Mistral
- DeepSeek
- Qwen

Exact model identifiers are recorded in [`config/models.json`](config/models.json) and in every run manifest. A model substitution requires a methodology version change or a separately labelled run.

The panel captures variation across developers. It does not represent market share among founders.

## 7. Conditions

### 7.1 Closed-book baseline

Tools and live web search are disabled. The system instruction tells the model to rely on knowledge already available to it. This condition measures observable model behaviour consistent with accumulated model knowledge. It cannot inspect model weights directly.

### 7.2 Open-book baseline

Live search is enabled and citations are requested. The controlled open-book comparison uses the same Exa retrieval engine, result limit and search instruction across all 8 model families. This isolates differences in model selection and synthesis while holding the retrieval layer approximately constant. It does not reproduce the native consumer experience of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or other products.

The open-book comparison contains 3 responses per model-prompt cell, producing 480 planned responses across the frozen 20-prompt panel.

Closed-book and open-book findings must not be combined into a single visibility statistic.

### 7.3 Specificity ladder

A separate prompt-sensitivity experiment tests when smaller or specialist firms enter the answer. Each ladder holds the underlying founder category constant while adding relevant coordinates:

1. Broad category
2. Category plus stage, geography and business model
3. Category plus stage, geography, business model, capital profile and a specific operating constraint

The experiment measures whether increasing legitimate founder specificity changes firm concentration, specialist inclusion and prestige leakage. The wording is frozen before results are collected.

These outcomes are operationalised before collection as follows:

- **Concentration:** the share of firm recommendations captured by the five most-mentioned firms at each specificity level.
- **Prestige leakage:** the share captured by the baseline prestige pool. The pool contains every non-empty firm appearing in at least four of the ten closed-book discovery categories: Andreessen Horowitz, Battery Ventures, Bessemer Venture Partners, Index Ventures and Lux Capital. This is an empirical breadth rule, not an editorial list of famous firms.
- **Target association:** inclusion of a non-prestige firm that appeared in at least ten of the 40 baseline responses for the corresponding discovery category and achieved its own highest baseline inclusion count in that category. This reproducible screen is a proxy for category association, not proof that a firm is genuinely specialist.
- **Verified specialist inclusion:** inclusion of a firm whose current stage, geography and category fit is confirmed from a primary source under the verification protocol below. This is reported separately from the mechanical target-association measure.

The five ladder categories map to the baseline discovery prompts for climate hardware, European defence, European university spinouts, developer infrastructure and capital-efficient SaaS. Ties in a firm's highest category count qualify. Firms with blank names are excluded from every rate.

### 7.4 Native-product spot check

A small descriptive check compares the API experiment with the consumer products founders may actually use. It covers ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok, subject to product access, using fresh chats and the exact frozen wording for four prompts:

- `discovery_enterprise_ai`
- `discovery_eu_defence`
- `problem_founder_led_sales`
- `problem_default_alive`

Each product-prompt pair is sampled once. The researcher records the displayed product or model label, account state, personalisation or memory state where visible, whether the product searched the web, the answer text, citations, timestamp and any refusal or error. No hidden model identity is inferred when the interface does not disclose it.

These 16 observations are a spot check, not a powered comparison. They are reported separately and never pooled with the controlled closed-book or common-retriever results because native products differ in system prompts, routing, personalisation, retrieval and interface behaviour.

## 8. Standard response instruction

Discovery prompts request no more than five firms and require a specific reason for each inclusion. Models are instructed to avoid famous generalists when they lack a concrete connection to the founder's situation.

Founder-problem prompts request no more than five firms or investors. Models must connect advice to published venture thinking and identify a source when known.

Responses use a structured schema containing:

- Firm
- Individual
- Reason
- Named framework
- Source title
- Source URL
- Self-reported confidence
- Honourable mentions
- Uncertainties

Self-reported confidence is not treated as calibrated probability.

## 9. Execution controls

- Every request starts with a fresh context.
- The system and user prompts remain identical across models, subject only to provider syntax.
- Temperature is set to `0.1` where supported.
- Output length is capped consistently at 3,000 completion tokens for baseline `v0.1a`.
- Reasoning effort is requested at `low` for OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI, Llama and Qwen. Reasoning is disabled for the tested DeepSeek and Mistral models because compatibility tests showed that the shared reasoning request exhausted the answer budget without returning content. This provider-level difference is recorded in each exact request payload.
- Search is disabled during the first baseline.
- Model fallbacks are disabled where supported.
- Failed calls are retried with exponential delay and retained in the error log.
- Completed model-prompt pairs are checkpointed and never intentionally rebilled during resume.
- Execution order is randomised from a recorded seed for the publication-grade run.
- The runner enforces a declared spending ceiling.

## 10. Repetition and stability

The 20-prompt, eight-model closed-book baseline contains 5 independent responses per model-prompt pair, producing 800 planned responses. Run 1 is retained as the initial baseline. Runs 2–5 are collected as a separately manifested replication batch before firm-level results are analysed.

Single responses are used for exploration. Claims about stable associations require repeated runs. Prompt-model cells that remain materially unstable after 5 runs may receive 5 additional confirmatory runs under a separately registered extension.

A result should be described as:

- **Observed once:** present in one sampled answer
- **Repeated:** present in multiple runs of the same model and prompt
- **Cross-model:** present across multiple developer families
- **Stable:** repeated within models and observed across model families

## 11. Measures

### 11.1 Mention rate

The share of eligible responses in which a firm or investor appears.

### 11.2 Top-position rate

The share of eligible responses in which a firm appears as the first recommendation.

### 11.3 Cross-model consensus

The number of model families that include a firm for the same prompt.

### 11.4 Category ownership

Repeated, specific association between a firm and an investment category across prompts, models or runs.

### 11.5 Problem ownership

Repeated association between a firm or investor and a founder operating problem.

### 11.6 Specificity

The degree to which the explanation provides verifiable reasons connected to the prompt. Specificity is coded separately from prominence.

### 11.7 Prestige leakage

The share of non-empty recommendations belonging to the empirically defined baseline prestige pool in Section 7.3. Whether the accompanying rationale is generic, weakly related or unsupported is a separate human-coded judgment and is not part of this quantitative rate.

### 11.8 Attribution fidelity

Whether a named framework or idea is connected to the correct originator and original source.

### 11.9 Retrieval gap

The difference between a firm's closed-book inclusion and open-book inclusion for the same prompt panel.

### 11.10 Retrieval convergence (exploratory)

After reviewing partial open-book output, the researchers added an exploratory diagnostic for whether a common retrieval layer causes different model families to cite the same domains or recommend the same newly retrieved firms. This analysis reports source-domain frequency, the number of model families citing each domain, and cross-model recommendation overlap within prompts.

This measure was added after some open-book results were visible. It is post-hoc, is labelled exploratory in every result, and is not evidence for a preregistered hypothesis. It does not change the frozen prompts, response collection or preregistered measures.

## 12. Human verification

Model output is evidence of model behaviour. It is not evidence that a factual claim about a venture firm is true.

Before publication, researchers must verify:

- Current investment stage and geography
- Sector focus
- Relevant portfolio examples
- Named partner affiliation
- Framework origin and wording
- Original publication source
- Whether a cited URL supports the claim

Primary firm sources, original publications and dated portfolio records are preferred. Every published correction or verification should preserve source URL, access date and reviewer.

## 13. Analysis safeguards

- Hypotheses and measures are recorded before complete baseline analysis.
- Raw observations remain distinct from manually coded judgments.
- Aggregate tables must be reproducible from reviewed data.
- Missing responses remain missing and are not silently imputed.
- Provider errors and refusals are reported.
- Negative and null findings are retained.
- Model recommendations are described as recommendations by tested systems at a specific date.
- The project does not label any firm objectively best.

## 14. Public-release policy

The public repository may contain:

- Methodology and version history
- Exact prompts and model configuration
- Reproduction code
- Reviewed aggregate data
- Sanitised response excerpts within applicable usage terms
- Verification sources
- Corrections and limitations

The repository must not contain:

- API keys or environment files
- Private calls, client information or unpublished business material
- Unreviewed allegations or model-generated factual claims
- Local private paths or raw research notes

The first closed-book and open-book baselines should be collected before result pages are publicly indexed. This reduces contamination from the benchmark's own firm-category associations.

## 15. Longitudinal design

Future runs should retain the frozen prompt panel and publish:

- Run date
- Model changes
- Prompt-version changes
- Search-provider changes
- Repetition count
- Changes in firm inclusion and attribution

When prompts or scoring definitions change, results belong to a new benchmark version and should not be presented as directly comparable without an explicit bridge analysis.

## 16. Reflexivity

A public benchmark connecting firms to categories may itself become retrievable by AI systems. The project therefore becomes a possible intervention after publication.

Results are divided into:

- **Pre-publication baseline:** collected before firm-result data are released
- **Post-publication observations:** collected after the benchmark becomes available to retrieval systems

Any movement after publication may have many causes. The project will not claim that the repository caused a change without stronger evidence.

## 17. Interpretation standard

The benchmark can support claims such as:

> Six of eight tested model families recommended Firm A for this prompt in the September 2026 closed-book baseline.

It cannot support claims such as:

> Firm A is the best investor for companies in this category.

The distinction is mandatory in data labels, charts, headlines and commentary.
